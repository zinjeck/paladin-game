# Paladin food and household invariants

These rules define the current physical food loop and must remain consistent across needs, decision-making, hauling, household storage, and production.

## Personal hunger

- Hunger is capped at 100 and loses 40 points per citizen-day.
- Fish and meat each restore 20 hunger.
- Normal food seeking begins at 70 hunger; critical seeking begins at 30.
- Citizens calculate food demand toward 100 after subtracting nutrition already carried in personal inventory.
- Food remains a whole physical item. A citizen consumes an item only when its full nutrition fits without exceeding 100.
- A citizen at 70 with no food therefore seeks two 20-point items, eats one to reach 90, and carries the second until hunger reaches 80.

## Source choice

- Critical hunger chooses the fastest reachable legal food source.
- Normal hunger gives the citizen's own home a moderate routine preference only when its exact route costs no more than 125% of the fastest legal alternative.
- Equal-cost alternatives use the stable source order: home, stockpile, City Keep, workplace output, ordinary ground pile.
- Food-task reservations must prevent the same physical unit from being promised twice.

## Household and public reserves

- Each home targets one full citizen-day of food per resident: 40 nutrition, or two current food items, per resident.
- Public storage protects a separate half-day reserve per living citizen: 20 nutrition, or one current food item, per citizen.
- Ordinary pantry replenishment may begin only after the public reserve floor is satisfied.
- A resident may withdraw from their own home; unrelated citizens may not use that private pantry.
- Personal carried food does not count toward the household pantry target.

## Production balance

- A fully productive four-worker Fishing Grounds completes one fish per two worker-hours.
- Across the current nine-hour shift, that is 18 fish, enough to feed eight citizens at two fish per day while leaving a modest reserve-building surplus.
